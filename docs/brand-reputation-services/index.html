The reputation problems companies call us about

AI Narrative Blind Spot

Millions of people instead of scrolling through Google, now ask AI for recommendations, research, and opinions. If AI misinterprets your story or does not mention you at all — you are losing clients, money and trust

What's included in Strategic Information-Field Management

Why corporate reputation management has to run as one strategic program

Online reputation management used to mean page one of Google. AI search has changed the rules: it reads across your entire footprint at once — including the parts everyone assumed were dead. A five-year-old review, an old forum thread, a review site your team stopped checking years ago — any of it can resurface as the very first line an AI model gives someone asking about you today. Managing search but not reviews, or reviews without the narrative around them or AI search results, leaves exactly the gap that surfaces.
The payoff is direct: potential customers and partners find an accurate picture at the moment of making a decision. It defines whether you build trust or lose it.
Owned assets and positive coverage improve online visibility much better than removal of unwanted materials. And brand presence becomes defensible: a strong reputation absorbs a bad week.

Managing the online image proactively is what it means to truly protect your brand.
We take your whole information field under control: search, reviews, media, AI answers, narratives — so everything people find about you works for the business. Like insurance for your digital reputation.

Brand reputation management — or reputation management for business, if you're searching by outcome rather than category — is the ongoing practice of monitoring, protecting and shaping everything a company's market finds about it: search, reviews, media narrative, sentiment and AI-generated answers.It is the enterprise form of online reputation management, run with an owned narrative, a dedicated team and a measurement framework, rather than reacting to individual results as they appear. At Reputation House it is delivered as Strategic Information-Field Management, RH Control's full-program retainer.

SERM covers one surface: what ranks on Google, Bing and YouTube for your brand name. Online reputation management covers all of them, adding reviews, media narrative and AI answers. SERM is a workstream inside this program and also runs as a standalone retainer, as does the narrative and content layer on its own.

A working reputation strategy has four parts, and a reputation management strategy that skips any one of them leaks. Continuous reputation monitoring, so you learn about a problem before your customers do. A content strategy that builds owned assets worth ranking, supported by search engine optimization rather than replaced by it. Structured review management services, so ratings reflect the actual customer base. And a crisis protocol agreed in advance, so the first hour of a bad day is not spent deciding who speaks.
